===TITLE===
Matthew 10: Jesus Commissions His Disciples

===CATEGORIES===
Gospel of Matthew
Teachings of Jesus
Bible Study

===META_DESCRIPTION===
Explore Matthew 10 as Jesus empowers His disciples, emphasizing mission, discipleship costs, and the call to fearless confession.

===KEY_TAKEAWAYS===
- **Authority Shared**: Jesus empowers His disciples with His authority to heal and proclaim the Kingdom.
- **Focused Mission**: The initial mission targets Israel, emphasizing urgency and reliance on God.
- **Cost of Discipleship**: Following Jesus involves facing persecution and enduring hardships.
- **Fearless Confession**: Believers are called to openly confess Christ, trusting in God's providence.
- **Receiving Christ**: Welcoming Jesus' messengers is akin to welcoming Christ Himself.

===BODY===
## Sharing Jesus' Authority: The Twelve as a Renewed Israel

Matthew 10 begins with Jesus calling His twelve disciples and granting them authority over unclean spirits and diseases. This act is significant, as it establishes the Twelve not only as His followers but as His representatives, echoing the twelve tribes of Israel. This signifies a renewal of Israel through Jesus and His disciples, who are tasked with extending His ministry.

> "And he called to him his twelve disciples and gave them authority over unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal every disease and every affliction."

The Twelve are empowered to proclaim the Kingdom and perform miracles, continuing Jesus' work. This delegation of authority underscores their role as Jesus' official messengers, carrying His message and power wherever they go.

## A Focused, Urgent, and Dependent Mission

### Focused on "the lost sheep of Israel"

Jesus instructs His disciples to focus their mission on the "lost sheep of Israel," marking an initial step in God's redemptive plan. This focus does not neglect the Gentiles but highlights the urgency of calling Israel back to its covenantal role.

> "Go nowhere among the Gentiles and enter no town of the Samaritans, but go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel."

### Traveling light: dependence on God and His people

Jesus instructs His disciples to travel without extra provisions, emphasizing reliance on God's provision through the hospitality of others. This fosters dependence on God and ensures the disciples' mission remains focused and unencumbered.

> "Acquire no gold or silver or copper for your belts... For the laborer deserves his food."

## Sheep Among Wolves: The Cost of Discipleship

Jesus warns His disciples about the challenges they will face, including persecution and betrayal. He uses the metaphor of sheep among wolves to describe their vulnerability and instructs them to be wise and innocent.

> "Behold, I am sending you out as sheep in the midst of wolves, so be wise as serpents and innocent as doves."

Despite the hardships, Jesus assures them of the Holy Spirit's guidance, the promise of salvation through endurance, and the strategic continuation of their mission.

## Fearless Confession in a Hostile World

Jesus calls His disciples to fearless confession, reminding them that opposition is expected but should not deter their mission. He emphasizes God's providence, assuring them of their value and care under God's watchful eye.

> "So have no fear of them, for nothing is covered that will not be revealed... Fear not, therefore; you are of more value than many sparrows."

Public acknowledgment of Jesus is crucial, as He promises to acknowledge those who confess Him before the Father.

## Division, Cross-Bearing, and Ultimate Allegiance

Jesus speaks of the division His message brings, even within families, underscoring the radical allegiance required to follow Him. He calls His disciples to take up their cross, symbolizing the willingness to endure suffering and even death for His sake.

> "And whoever does not take his cross and follow me is not worthy of me. Whoever finds his life will lose it, and whoever loses his life for my sake will find it."

## Receiving the Messengers, Receiving the King

The chapter concludes by emphasizing the significance of receiving Jesus' messengers. To welcome them is to welcome Jesus Himself, and even small acts of kindness towards them are eternally significant.

> "Whoever receives you receives me, and whoever receives me receives him who sent me."

This teaching highlights the interconnectedness of Jesus' mission through His disciples and the importance of supporting those who carry His message. This passage encourages believers to embrace the mission with courage and faith, knowing that their efforts are valued and rewarded by God.

## Conclusion: Embracing the Mission

Matthew 10 presents a realistic yet hopeful vision of discipleship. The chapter balances the challenges of following Jesus with the assurance of His presence and reward. Jesus' instructions to His disciples serve as a timeless reminder of the cost and commitment required in the Christian journey. As believers, we are called to embrace this mission with courage, trusting in God's provision and the eternal significance of our actions.

===FAQ===
**What is the main focus of Matthew 10?**
Matthew 10 focuses on Jesus commissioning His disciples, sharing His authority with them, and instructing them on their mission, emphasizing reliance on God, the cost of discipleship, and the necessity of fearless confession.

**Why does Jesus limit the disciples' mission to Israel?**
The initial focus on Israel reflects a strategic priority in God's redemptive plan, emphasizing the urgency of calling God's covenant people back to their vocation before the mission expands to all nations.
