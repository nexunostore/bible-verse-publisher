===BODY===
## The Big Question: Who May Dwell with God?

Psalm 15 opens with a profound question: who is fit to dwell in God's presence? This question is framed through the imagery of the "tabernacle" and "holy hill," representing the heart of worship in ancient Israel. This psalm serves as a self-examination for worshipers approaching God's sanctuary, encouraging them to align their lives with His holiness.

The imagery of dwelling with God is not just about a physical location but about a spiritual state. It invites us to reflect on our relationship with God and the kind of life that is pleasing to Him. To dwell with God is to live in a way that is consistent with His character, seeking to embody His righteousness and love in all aspects of life.

## The Inner Life: Integrity and Truth

> "He that walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness, and speaketh the truth in his heart."

This verse emphasizes the importance of consistency in our daily walk, the active pursuit of righteousness, and internal honesty. True worship involves a life without division between our spiritual and real-world selves. Integrity is not just about outward actions but about the truth that resides within our hearts.

Living with integrity means that our actions align with our beliefs, and our words reflect our inner truth. It is about being genuine and transparent, allowing God's truth to guide our decisions and interactions.

## The Tongue: Refusing to Wound with Words

> "He that backbiteth not with his tongue, nor doeth evil to his neighbour, nor taketh up a reproach against his neighbour."

Psalm 15 highlights the power of words, urging us to avoid slander and harm. Our speech should reflect God's holiness, refraining from gossip and character assassination. Words have the power to build up or tear down, and as followers of Christ, we are called to use our words to encourage and uplift others.

Choosing words that heal rather than harm is a reflection of God's love in us. It requires us to be mindful of how we speak and to prioritize kindness and truth in our communication.

## The Moral Compass: What and Whom You Honor

> "In whose eyes a vile person is contemned; but he honoureth them that fear the LORD. He that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not."

This verse calls for moral clarity, honoring those who fear the Lord and keeping promises even when it is costly. Our values and commitments should mirror God's character. It challenges us to evaluate who we admire and why, encouraging us to honor those who live in reverence to God.

Honoring what God honors means standing firm in our convictions, even when it is inconvenient or challenging. It is about being faithful to our commitments and valuing integrity over personal gain.

## Money and Power: Justice in Economic Life

> "He that putteth not out his money to usury, nor taketh reward against the innocent."

Psalm 15 addresses the ethical use of money and power, rejecting exploitation and corruption. Fairness and justice should guide our economic interactions, reflecting God's heart for the vulnerable. In a world where financial gain often comes at the expense of others, this psalm calls us to a higher standard of justice and fairness.

Using our resources to uplift others and act justly in our financial dealings is a testament to God's justice. It is about recognizing the responsibility that comes with power and using it to serve rather than exploit.

## The Promise: Unshakable Stability

> "He that doeth these things shall never be moved."

The psalm concludes with a promise of stability for those who live according to God's standards. While life may bring challenges, a life anchored in God remains secure and unshakable. This promise offers hope and assurance that living in alignment with God's will brings peace and stability.

Living a Psalm 15 life means embracing these principles in our daily lives, allowing them to shape our actions and relationships. It is a call to live authentically and faithfully, trusting in God's promises and finding our security in Him.

## Bringing It Home: Living Psalm 15 Today

Psalm 15 invites us to live near God through four key areas: character, speech, loyalties, and the use of power and money. It transforms worship from a Sunday activity into a daily lifestyle. As we draw closer to God, these qualities should naturally emerge in our lives.

In Christ, we find both our security and our calling, enabling us to pursue the Psalm 15 life with humility and hope. This psalm is a reminder that our relationship with God is not confined to rituals but is expressed through our daily actions and choices.
